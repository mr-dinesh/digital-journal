## Hi, I'm Dinesh.

I work in cybersecurity. That's the job where you spend years building defences, and then watch someone click a phishing link anyway.

I came to security through the military, which turns out to be decent preparation. In both, what gets you is never the threat you planned for.

By day I think about risk, rules and what to do when things go wrong. By night I build small things with AI, read code nobody asked me to read, and post Kannada philosophy to Mastodon.

---

## What's here

**Vibecoding:** I'm working through 100 small projects built with AI, and writing about each one: what I wanted, what I got, and what went sideways along the way. Some took an afternoon. Some took until 2am. I'm not at 100 yet. Not even close.

**Notes:** shorter technical write-ups, like reading curl's source code (slow going, entirely self-inflicted), rescuing an old tablet, or making my computer start up faster.

**Journal:** personal writing. A marathon, a mentor, things worth remembering.

**Reading:** notes on books that changed how I think about something.

**Links:** a page a day of things I found worth your time, sent from my phone.

---

## The longer version

I'm from Bengaluru. I spent years in uniform before landing in tech, so I was doing risk management long before I had a job title for it.

I started this blog because writing forces me to be clear and building things forces me to be honest. The vibecoding series answers a question I kept asking: what can these AI tools actually do with a real problem? So far: more than I expected, in ways I didn't expect, and never quite on the first try. A bit like me.

Outside work I read a lot: history, economics, long magazine pieces. I have a soft spot for D.V. Gundappa's *Mankutimmana Kagga*, 945 verses of Kannada philosophy from the 1940s that wrestle with life's big questions without pretending to answer them. Good company.
